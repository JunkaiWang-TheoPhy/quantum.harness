\begin{abstract}
This supplement records a six-hour, proof-directed HPC sprint for Issue 128.
Before using any new HPC result, a post-freeze D5 sidecar was integrated into
the global error ledger and independently verified.  It accepts 95 steps,
rejects 94, and certifies the exact resource ratio
$11791/2851=4.135741844966678\ldots$.  The production phase computes exact
right-generator coefficients through complementary monolithic and 31-way
stage-sharded paths.  Every accepted artifact is canonical, SHA-256-bound,
source-identified, and checked by a verifier that can reject malformed
coverage, algebra, provenance, boundary, or resource arithmetic.  No
fivefold result is asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Certified result and research gate}

For the periodic $12\times12$ isotropic Heisenberg benchmark at $T=1$ and
global operator-norm tolerance $10^{-6}$, the certified fourth-order Suzuki
post-freeze result accepts $r=95$ and rejects $r=94$.  With
$G(r)=30r+1$ merged group exponentials, the exact resource ratio is
\[
  \frac{G(393)}{G(95)}=\frac{11791}{2851}
  =4.135741844966678\ldots .
\]
The exact global bounds are $9.90449140283245\times10^{-7}$ at 95 and
$1.04680611656037\times10^{-6}$ at 94.  The D4 and D5 sidecars contain 75,324
and 605,832 canonical Pauli terms, respectively, and both are independently
checked for complete coverage and pairwise anticommutation within every
group.  The original ten-file package remains frozen at 97 steps and is not
silently rewritten.

Fivefold arithmetic would require $r\le78$, but the present D4 contribution
alone is already approximately $1.259084\times10^{-6}$ there.  Thus an exact
D8 coefficient, even if zero, cannot close fivefold performance under the
current nonnegative ledger.  A new multiplier may be reported only after a
complete global ledger and independent fast/deep verification succeed.

\section{Exact sharding algorithm}

For stages $V_j(t)=\exp(-i a_j t H_{c_j})$ in the fixed 31-stage formula,
linearity gives a decomposition of the right generator into 31 contributions.
Each array task starts from $a_jH_{c_j}$ and conjugates it through all later
stages, truncating only the formal series degree at eight.  Coefficients live
exactly in $\mathbb{Q}[\alpha]/(\alpha^3-4)$; Pauli operators are canonicalized
under the $2\times2$ translation cell before serialization.

The shard format does not expose process-local registry bit indices.  Every
Pauli string is encoded by physical integer coordinates and labels.  Canonical
JSON and gzip with fixed metadata make repeated output byte-deterministic.
The manifest records stage, order, timestamps, term counts, peak RSS, source
commit, dirty flag, and SHA-256.  The reducer rejects a missing stage, wrong
formula/order, mixed source state, corrupt hash, or duplicate stage.  It also
requires forward and reverse exact reduction to produce identical maps.

For a cubic coefficient $c=a_0+a_1\alpha+a_2\alpha^2$, an outward rational
interval for $\alpha=\sqrt[3]{4}$ encloses $c$.  Summing the larger endpoint
magnitudes yields a rigorous Pauli-$\ell_1$ upper bound.  This bound is a
screening ingredient; it is not by itself the global $r=78$ certificate.

\section{Verification evidence}

The final local regression gate reported 112 passing tests and 11 deselected
slow tests in 175.21 seconds.  The D5-integrated fast verifier returned
\texttt{valid=true}; the deep verifier regenerated every D4/D5 coefficient
from the 31-stage formula, returned
\texttt{deep\_proof\_regenerated=true}, and completed in 167.62 seconds with
1.53~GB peak RSS.  A detached clean worktree repeated the fast verifier,
focused certificate tests, and every delivery SHA-256 check.

The independent remote verification job is recorded without hiding a mixed
outcome: its deep verifier passed, while the encompassing pytest command
returned nonzero.  Five failures were caused by the synchronized remote tree
lacking usable Git metadata; one pre-existing slow symplectic discovery test
returned a different numeric value.  This remote pytest result is not called
``all green'' and is not used to replace the successful local certificate
gate.

The exact-D8 array provides a useful scale check.  Stage 25 completed in
7349.722 seconds with 17,211,714 degree-eight terms and 52.15~GB peak RSS;
its output digest is
\texttt{c64c0729a1ef\ldots a95a4eb3cf7b}; the full digest is retained in the
machine-readable sprint log.
The order-six fallback uses the same stage-shard contract but truncates at D6.
Its reducer requires all 31 manifests, verifies each payload digest, and
requires forward and reverse exact sums to agree.  A compact parent manifest
binds every source shard and the reducer implementation before the exact D6
sidecar can enter the main certificate.

An additional cross-order replay job compared the degree-six maps embedded in
the independently generated order-six and order-eight shards for stages
25--30.  All six exact maps agreed term by term, covering 710,298, 446,094,
150,858, 2,028, 48, and zero terms, respectively.  This check compares
canonical Pauli keys and exact cubic-field coefficients rather than only a
reported norm.

The matching-order array is a separate discovery lane.  Its JSON records
\texttt{trusted=false} and states that its purpose is discovery ranking.  A
favorable ranking can nominate a formula for exact reconstruction but cannot
enter the certificate directly.

The 25 heavy D8 array cells remained in \texttt{RUNNING} state beyond
4:15:00 and ultimately reached \texttt{TIMEOUT} after 4:30:11--4:30:13 of
observed wall time.  The other six cells completed.  Thus the requested
4.25-hour production floor is supported by \texttt{sacct}, not inferred from
a requested time limit.  Because the full array was not successful, Slurm
cancelled its \texttt{afterok} reducer; no partial sum was reported globally.

At 07:13 CST a complementary 16-way E7 word array, job 23044736, began from
byte-identical local and remote sources.  It partitions all 16,380 degree-seven
free words into disjoint index intervals.  Dependent job 23044752 validates
complete interval coverage, source and payload hashes, and forward/reverse
exact reduction before forming
$D_6=7E_7+\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{ad}_A^2(E_5)$.  Its result remains
provisional until the complete artifact and verifier gates pass.

The first minute of the 16-way lane exposed load imbalance: three contiguous
blocks finished in roughly 43--45 seconds with zero E7 contribution, while
the other blocks entered expensive words.  Per-process RSS was only about
165--203~MiB despite a 64~GiB allocation.  A CPU-saturating fallback therefore
launched as job 23044754: each of 16 array cells uses \texttt{srun} to run 17
single-core evaluators, partitioning the same word set into 272 disjoint
intervals.  All 16 cells began within 11 seconds and all 272 running manifests
were observed.  Job 23044755 reduces only that lane and retains the same
coverage, hash, bidirectional-sum, and exact-identity gates.

After about five minutes the 272-way lane had 155 complete shards, 117 still
running, and no failures, with the unfinished set concentrated in expensive
word ranges.  A second independent lane, job 23044779, therefore halves the
range size again to 544 shards across 32 array cells and 544 active Python
evaluators.  All cells entered \texttt{RUNNING} within 11 seconds and emitted
their manifests.  Reducer 23044780 reads only this lane.  If both reductions
finish, their exact D6 maps must agree term by term before either can support a
certificate.

At roughly four minutes the 544-way lane had completed 383 shards with no
failure; completed high-cost shards had reached about 3~GiB peak RSS.  A final
1,088-way lane, job 23044815, reduced each range to about 15 words and started
all 64 cells (1,088 evaluators).  Reducer 23044816 is directory-isolated from
the other lanes.  The first complete result wins time-to-answer, while any
additional complete result supplies an exact cross-partition comparison.

A consolidated 07:28:33 CST snapshot recorded 1,748 allocated CPUs in running
jobs for the account.  The 272-, 544-, and 1,088-way E7 lanes had completed
242, 482, and 567 shards, respectively, with zero failed shards.  Their maximum
completed-shard RSS values were about 6.41, 4.15, and 1.33~GiB.  Thus the finer
partition increased active evaluator concurrency while reducing individual
memory and tail exposure; the reported figure is scheduler state, not a sum of
requested limits.

Before either fanout reducer became eligible, deployment preflight found that
its Slurm wrapper still passed a literal shard count of 16.  The wrapper was
changed to consume the exported \texttt{ISSUE128\_SHARD\_COUNT} while retaining
16 as the default, passed \texttt{bash -n}, was synchronized byte-for-byte, and
was inspected remotely.  Jobs 23044755 and 23044780 remained dependency-pending
throughout the correction and were submitted with counts 272 and 544,
respectively; no reduction had begun with the stale parameter.

Slurm snapshots a batch script at submission.  Consequently, the already
submitted 544-way reducer 23044780 still started with the old literal count and
failed closed in two seconds when shard 0 declared 544 rather than 16; its
output directory remained empty.  Replacement 23044906 was submitted from the
corrected wrapper and immediately ran with a 45-minute limit.  The still-pending
old 272 reducer 23044755 was cancelled before execution and replaced by
23044907 with the original parent dependency.  The 1,088-way reducer 23044816
had been submitted after the correction and passed the metadata gate.

\section{Final adjudication protocol}

The outcome will be classified as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If all 31 shards and exact reduction succeed, insert the resulting D8
        upper bound into a complete $r=78$ ledger and rerun fast/deep checks.
  \item If any task reaches OOM or walltime, retain successful shards and
        scheduler evidence, retry only missing cells if time remains, and make
        no inference from a partial sum.
  \item If the complete ledger is at most $10^{-6}$, prepare a new independently
        bound certificate before describing a fivefold result.
  \item Otherwise publish the strict negative result or remaining numerical
        gap, while keeping the certified 4.135742-fold result unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Observed final outcome}

The original D8 array ended with six completed and 25 timed-out cells; each
heavy cell recorded 4:30:11--4:30:13 of actual elapsed time.  Its reducer did
not run.  The monolithic D8 fallback timed out at 4:15:29.  The monolithic D6
path timed out at 3:30:05, and its 31-way stage array ended with 13 completed
and 18 timed-out cells.  Neither path emitted a complete global coefficient
map.

The CPU-saturating E7 parents did close completely: the 272-, 544-, and
1,088-shard lanes completed all 16, 32, and 64 Slurm cells, respectively, with
complete word coverage and no failed shard manifest.  Their subsequent D6
reducers reached walltime at 40:07, 45:06, and 24:21.  Because output is written
atomically, no partial \texttt{degree-6.json.gz} was left behind.  The dependent
cross-lane comparisons and certificate integration therefore did not run.
The original 16-way E7 lane ended with four completed and 12 timed-out cells.
The serial direct-identity lane timed out at 1:39:06 after reaching word 1,792
of 16,380.  At 08:24:40 CST the user queue was empty; no sprint job remained
beyond the bounded execution window.

The final local regression gate was 112 passed and 11 deselected in 175.21
seconds.  Operationally, the sprint proved the requested 4.25-hour production
floor, exercised up to 1,748 running CPU allocations, and produced complete
hash-bound E7 word partitions, but did not close a new D6 or D8 reduction
within six hours.  This is recorded as a strict negative operational result,
not converted into a numerical claim.

\textbf{Claim status.}  The currently accepted result is the independently
verified 4.1357418449-fold D5-integrated certificate.  The D6/D8 work is
research evidence only until its artifacts, exact ledger, adjacent-step
boundary, hashes, and deep regeneration all pass.
